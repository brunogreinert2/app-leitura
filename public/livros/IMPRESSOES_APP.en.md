This is a reader for classical texts that runs **entirely on your device**: after the first visit it needs no internet at all. Tap any heading below to open that topic — in the body of a text every heading starts closed and opens with one tap.

# Install the app on your device

This site **is** the app: installing means getting its own icon (the Greek letter Φ) and opening full screen, without looking like a browser. Once installed, it works offline.

## On iPhone and iPad

Only **Safari** installs apps of this kind (Apple's rule — Chrome on iPhone cannot).

1. Open this address in **Safari**
2. Tap the **Share** button (the square with an arrow, at the bottom)
3. Scroll the list and tap **"Add to Home Screen"**
4. Confirm the name and tap **Add**

## On Android

1. Open this address in **Chrome**
2. Tap the **⋮** menu (three dots, top corner)
3. Tap **"Install app"** or **"Add to home screen"**
4. Confirm

On some devices Chrome offers the install in a banner at the bottom — just accept it.

## On a computer

1. Open this address in **Chrome** or **Edge**
2. On the right of the address bar an **install** icon appears (a monitor with an arrow, or "⊕")
3. Click it and confirm

The app becomes its own window, with a shortcut in the start menu / desktop.

# How to use the reader

## Reading

Tap any heading to open or close that section. Headings further left are the larger chapters; the further right, the deeper in the structure of the text.

Footnote marks (small numbers in the text) open the note right there, in a box — tap it, then "Back to the text". Holding your finger on the mark takes you to the full list of notes.

Names underlined with dots are **figures**: one tap opens a card with the entry (like Wikipedia); holding your finger opens the entry as a full page — the ← arrow returns to where you were.

## Text size

Use the **+** and **−** buttons at the top, or pinch with two fingers over the text. The text grows and reflows to the width of the screen without losing the point where you were reading. There is no timid limit: one word per screen is a valid size.

## Colours and contrast

In the contents (Ξ) tap **Appearance** and choose the scheme that rests your eyes: sepia, parchment, black on white, white on black, yellow on black, green on black, or shades of blue. The choice is saved on the device.

## Searching

Tap the magnifier 🔍 and type:

- **a word** — the app sweeps the whole book, even with sections closed, ignoring accents ("misericordia" finds "misericórdia");
- **a reference** — `Gn 1:1`, `Ps 23:1`, `1Cor 13:4` in the Bible; `5.1` in the Encheiridion. Enter jumps straight to the address. Each work uses its own abbreviation, shown on its details card.

## Contents and library

The **Ξ** button (Greek xi) opens the book's **contents**, with its chapters and actions. The **Φ** button (Greek phi, the app's mark) opens the **library**, with the folder structure and a search by title, author or folder.

## Getting around with the keyboard

If you do not use a mouse, there are two movements, and the difference between them is what makes the screen navigable:

- **Tab** moves **within** a region, from one control to the next. **Shift+Tab** goes back.
- **F6** moves **between** regions: top bar, text, library, contents. **Shift+F6** goes the other way.

Without F6, getting from the text to a setting would mean crossing everything in between. With it, it is one key.

The first tab stop on the page is **Skip to the text**, which jumps the whole bar. **Esc** closes any panel or dialog and returns focus to the button that opened it.

## Listening to the text

The 🔊 button at the bottom opens the voice reader: **Listen** reads aloud what is open on screen, starting from where you stopped, highlighting the spoken paragraph. You can pause, resume, stop, choose the voice (the ones on your device) and adjust the speed. It all happens on the device — it works offline with the installed voices.

The voice changes by itself according to the language of each passage: Greek is read with a Greek voice, Hebrew with a Hebrew voice, English with an English voice. When the right voice is missing from the device, the app says so and uses the closest one — Latin, which no system has, comes out in Italian.

## Marking the language of a line

Sometimes the app has no way to guess: a two-word line, a stray verse, an interlinear alternating Portuguese and English line by line. In those cases you write the language at the end of the line, with the same `^` used for anchors — `In the beginning was the Word. ^eng` makes that line come out in an English voice.

The codes are `^por`, `^eng`, `^lat`, `^grc`, `^heb` and `^rus`. The mark does not appear in the reading, and what is written always beats what the app would guess. A line can carry both — address and language, in this order: `Text. ^gn-1-1 ^eng`. Any other anchor remains just an address, as always.

## Your own texts

In the library (Φ) there are two buttons:

- **+ Add files** — brings `.md` or `.txt` files from your device into the app.
- **✏ New text** — opens a blank sheet to **type or paste** anything: a conversation, an article, a note. Once saved, it becomes reading with everything the app offers — zoom, themes, collapsible headings, search.

Your texts live in the "My files" folder, **on your device only** (they are never uploaded anywhere), work offline, and can be edited later: open the text, tap Ξ and then **✏ Edit**. Books from the library cannot be edited — the collection is untouchable.

## Copying

In the contents (Ξ), **"Copy book"** copies the whole file, byte for byte — front matter, anchors and all. It is the faithful copy, meant for you to paste into an editor, work on it, and return the file to its folder.

In the **⋯** menu there is the passage copy, with two options: **visible only**, which ignores closed sections and comes out clean of markup, and **everything**, which comes out as it is in the file.

# About this project

This reader is part of the **Pedra Angular** project: a corpus of classical texts — ancient and modern philosophy, Scripture — preserved in plain text, made to last and to be read by anyone, on any device, with any eyesight.

The collection is polyglot: each work stays in the language it was written or translated into, and the app never translates it on the fly. Only the interface follows your device's language.

The embedded books are in the public domain or translated by the project itself. The app collects nothing, asks for no account, shows no advertising: it is just you and the text.
